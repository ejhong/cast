Moving a very large stone

Breaking delivery into small loads avoids transporting the completed block as one object.

Still to explainCasting does not remove mass, lifting work, mixing labor, or the need to obtain suitable ingredients.

02

A distant quarry

If suitable local inputs exist, a cast block could use them. If its aggregate comes from a distant quarry, that material still needs transport.

Still to explainA quarry match and a transport distance must be evaluated for the particular block; distance alone is not evidence of casting.

03

A finished object wider than its entrance

Formwork and small loads could enter separately and be assembled inside.

Still to explainThe object's date, installation sequence, chemistry, and available workspace must support that scenario.

FROM POSSIBILITY TO EVIDENCE

How would
we tell?

The proposal

Joseph Davidovits proposes that some Egyptian blocks were made from re-agglomerated limestone. Modern demonstrations establish a stone-making possibility; identifying ancient examples is a separate materials question.

The comparison

Quarried masonry, transported with organized labor and lifting systems, is the conventional account. Petrographic comparisons have also challenged the casting interpretation.

A USEFUL PHYSICAL TEST

Track the complete work needed for a specified block: sourcing, ingredient transport, water, mixing, mold reuse, curing, and strength. Compare both the logistics and the microstructure with the relevant quarried stone.
