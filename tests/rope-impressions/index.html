STAGE 01

A surface that can still yield.

Begin with a workable specimen and a rope laid above it. The curved route is chosen for this experiment; it is not a tracing of an archaeological feature.

ABOUT THIS MODELAn invented contact experiment, not a reconstruction of an identified ancient mark. The surface approximates the negative of the modeled strands, with slightly smoothed edges; depth and timing are illustrative. It does not simulate material flow or rope deformation.

THE EXPLANATORY IDEA

What could this
make easier?

A twisted rope touches workable material. Its strands leave a different surface from a smooth cord: a repeating pattern inside the groove.

01

A curved mark in hard stone

Contact before hardening can transfer a curved shape without carving that curve into already hard material.

Still to explainLater rope abrasion, tool marks, weathering, and impressions in a younger coating can also produce surface features.

02

Fine detail inside a groove

A yielding surface could copy the rope's strand spacing and direction of twist.

Still to explainA candidate needs a measured surface profile and a documented archaeological context. This scene supplies neither an ancient example nor its date.

03

Separating shape from process

Changing only the rope makes a simple comparison: a broad channel versus a channel with strand detail.

Still to explainReplicas and alternative processes must be compared under the same imaging conditions before a mark can diagnose its cause.

FROM POSSIBILITY TO EVIDENCE

How would
we tell?

The proposal

This atlas proposes a contact experiment: a rope could impress material that was workable at the time of contact. That could be a cast body, a surface layer, or a later repair; the impression alone does not distinguish them.

The comparison

Compare an impression with a rope dragged across hard stone, deliberate tool grooves, and natural surface damage. A resemblance in a photograph is a starting point for measurement.

A USEFUL PHYSICAL TEST

Press both smooth cord and twisted rope into a candidate mixture. Record pressure and release time; scan the cured surfaces. Compare them with abrasion controls and a measured ancient feature, including the direction and spacing of any strand detail.
