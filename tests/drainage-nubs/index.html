THE EXPLANATORY IDEA

What could this
make easier?

Open a low drain in a cloth-lined mold. Water escapes, the fabric bows outward, and the retained material leaves a protrusion when it hardens.

01

Protrusions left on a finished face

A cloth-covered outlet offers a way for a nub to form as part of the block, without carving the whole face back around it.

Still to explainThis still needs a physical replication that matches the shape, texture, and internal continuity of a particular ancient nub.

02

Why only some blocks?

A selective-drain scenario would add an outlet only where the mix needed drainage.

Still to explainSelective drainage is an extension explored here, not a demonstrated historical practice or a claim that all casting recipes need drains.

FROM POSSIBILITY TO EVIDENCE

How would
we tell?

The proposal

Fóti proposes a fabric-drainage origin for some stone protrusions. This study isolates the geometry and adds a selectable drain; it does not assume that every nub has the same origin.

The comparison

Bosses left during dressing, handling features, and unfinished surfaces offer other explanations. Their relevance depends on the individual stone and construction context.

A USEFUL PHYSICAL TEST

Cast matched specimens with a closed form, an open hole, and a cloth-covered hole. Record water loss, fabric deflection, curing, and nub shape. Compare internal structure and surface weave with documented examples.
