FOLLOW THE MAKING

STAGE 01

Put the precision in the form.

Timber sides and removable inserts define the outside edges and recessed faces. Making an accurate, reusable form is the first engineering challenge.

ABOUT THIS MODELAn invented mold around a simplified H-block. This is a geometric study, not a measured reconstruction of a particular artifact.

THE EXPLANATORY IDEA

What could this
make easier?

Watch a multipart mold separate from a stepped H-block. The proposed trick is to make the difficult geometry in the form, while the stone is still workable.

01

Sharp internal corners

An insert can form a recess while the material is soft, reducing the amount of hard-stone removal needed afterward.

Still to explainThe insert itself still needs accurate manufacture and a practical release direction.

02

Repeated geometry

Reusing a mold could reproduce both the intended shape and small accidental details.

Still to explainSimilar shapes alone do not show mold reuse. Surveyed dimensions and repeated defects would be more informative.

FROM POSSIBILITY TO EVIDENCE

How would
we tell?

The proposal

Davidovits and colleagues propose an artificial origin for sampled Pumapunku andesite. This study explores one possible molding arrangement; their materials claim does not establish this particular mold.

The comparison

Skilled stoneworking with templates, pecking, grinding, and abrasion can also produce regular geometry. The relevant comparison is the actual surface and internal structure of the stone.

A USEFUL PHYSICAL TEST

Make several blocks from the same multipart mold. Measure dimensional variation, release damage, seams, and repeated surface defects; compare them with documented H-blocks.
