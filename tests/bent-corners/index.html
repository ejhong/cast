THE ARCHITECTURAL QUESTION / KHAFRE’S VALLEY TEMPLE

A block that turns the corner.

The corner masonry at Khafre’s Valley Temple gives this study its question: could a flexible form make a block that continues around a right angle?

Egypt’s Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities describes the temple as a limestone structure encased in granite. A successful mold demonstration would still need a material that explains the actual granite; making the outline alone would not establish that connection.

Fóti’s sketches supply the proposed construction detail. Slides 5–6 show hide sheets enclosing slurry beside a recessed post. The animation develops that geometry into a freestanding specimen, with the supports and removal paths visible.

THE EXPLANATORY IDEA

What could this
make easier?

Wrap animal hides around timber posts, fill an L-shaped cavity, and release one continuous corner block. Explore the method in Marcell Fóti’s Bent corners sketches.

01

A single block around a corner

A continuous L-shaped cavity gives workable material both arms of the block at once. The inside angle is established by the supported hide.

Still to explainAn L-shaped outline can also be made by dressing a quarried stone. Shape alone does not identify the manufacturing process.

02

A flexible form with a sharp turn

Wrapping a sheet around a square post gives the flexible material a defined corner and a place to fasten its ends.

Still to explainHide stretch, seams, leakage, post bracing, and pressure on the longer spans need physical trials. The drawn sharp angle is a target, not a strength prediction.

03

Getting the form out afterward

The posts lift clear before the hides peel away from exposed faces. The corner post occupies an open recess, so its removal path passes beside the block.

Still to explainAdhesion, shrinkage around the post, release damage, and access in an actual wall could change the sequence. This specimen stands alone so those paths are visible.

FROM POSSIBILITY TO EVIDENCE

How would
we tell?

The proposal

Marcell Fóti’s Bent corners presentation proposes containing a slurry with animal hides fastened around posts. This study interprets the corner arrangement in his final two slides as an L-shaped mold. Filling, hardening, and removal extend those static sketches into a possible sequence; they are not documented ancient operations.

The comparison

Compare the proposal with shaping a quarried granite block to fit a corner, including dressing after placement. Survey the actual block and its joints, and compare tool traces, surface texture, mineral structure, and possible form-contact marks. A corner photograph alone cannot distinguish these processes.

A USEFUL PHYSICAL TEST

Build a small hide-and-post corner mold and document the mixture, sheet thickness, fastenings, and bracing. Measure deflection during filling, leakage, corner angle, shrinkage, and release damage. Compare the cured specimen with a dressed-stone control and a measured temple block, including their surface traces and internal mineral structure.
